## Objective

Construct a perceptron that compares two 10-bit integers, evaluate an analytically defined weight vector, and study whether a perceptron trained from examples learns the same comparison rule.

## Model and labels

Represent two unsigned 10-bit integers, $n_T$ and $n_B$, using a vector of $N=20$ components,

$$
\boldsymbol S=(S_1,\ldots,S_{20}),\qquad S_i\in\{-1,+1\}.
$$

The first ten entries encode $n_T$ and the remaining ten encode $n_B$. The desired classification is

$$
y=\begin{cases}
+1,&n_T>n_B,\\
-1,&n_T\leq n_B.
\end{cases}
$$

A perceptron with weights $\boldsymbol J$ predicts using the sign of its weighted sum:

$$
\widehat y=\operatorname{sign}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{20}S_iJ_i\right).
$$

**Tie convention:** The task specifies $-1$ when the two integers are equal. The implementation must therefore handle a zero weighted sum consistently with this label; the usual mathematical sign function, which returns zero at the origin, is not sufficient by itself.

## Reference and random perceptrons

Start from the specified *perfect* weight vector:

$$
J_i^*=2^{10-i}\quad (1\leq i\leq10),\qquad
J_i^*=-J_{i-10}^*\quad(11\leq i\leq20).
$$

Evaluate its error over many randomly generated number pairs, using a direct numerical comparison of the two integers as an independent benchmark.

Next, replace the reference weights with independent draws from a standard normal distribution, $J_i\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$, and estimate the random perceptron's test error against the reference comparison rule.

## Training experiments

1. Build two labeled training datasets of sizes **$P=500$** and **$P=2000$**.
2. Initialize a trainable perceptron and present labeled examples over successive passes through the corresponding dataset.
3. Update the weights **only for a misclassified example** according to

   $$
   J_i(t+1)=J_i(t)+\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\,y_\mu S_i^{\mu},\qquad N=20.
   $$

4. Calculate the training misclassification rate at the end of every full pass. Continue training until the training error reaches zero.
5. For each trained model, visualize the weight-dependent quantity

   $$
   \operatorname{sign}(J_i^*J_i)\,\log_2|J_i|
   $$

   against the weight index $i$, and discuss the resulting patterns.
6. Estimate the test error of the models trained with $P=500$ and $P=2000$ on a large sample of fresh integer pairs, comparing predictions with the perfect reference rule.

## Questions to investigate

- Why can a particular set of fixed weights implement an integer comparison?
- How different is the test error of a randomly initialized perceptron from that of the reference model?
- How does increasing the number of training examples affect the learned weights and generalization?
- How do training and test errors differ?
